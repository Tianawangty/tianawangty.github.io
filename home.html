I work with the economy’s pixels, from individual transactions to local markets. I use large-scale data to study how food prices change and how policy shapes them.

I’m a Ph.D. candidate in Agricultural and Applied Economics at the University of Georgia. I’m also pursuing an M.S. in Statistics. Before UGA, I spent three years as a research analyst at the International Monetary Fund. I supported country teams across southern Africa. Alongside that work, I pursued projects in crisis early warning and climate policy analysis, often using machine learning and data science. I hold an M.S. in Government Analytics from Johns Hopkins University and a B.B.A. in Applied Economics from Hong Kong Baptist University.
