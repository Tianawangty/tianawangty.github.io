The Food-Price Effect of the Agricultural Minimum Wage

Job market paper, with Michael Adjemian and Genti Kostandini

Farm labor is one of the costs behind a grocery bill. This paper studies how changes in the Adverse Effect Wage Rate, a wage floor used in the H-2A agricultural worker program, move through the food supply chain. It examines how much reaches retail prices and how farms and trade adjust along the way.

The analysis starts in the farm labor market, using county employment and payroll records to examine changes in jobs and labor spending. Next, state crop prices track what farmers receive, while product-level trade data track how imports adjust. At the retail stage, Nielsen scanner data measure changes in the prices shoppers pay.

The design combines changes in the agricultural wage premium with farms’ reliance on migrant labor measured in 2002. The premium measures how far the agricultural wage floor sits above the applicable general minimum wage. Production shares and farm-input links connect costs in producing states to downstream products. Local projections trace responses after each wage announcement, using monthly or quarterly estimates matched to each outcome.

Presented at the AAEA Annual Meeting, Kansas City, 2026.

Food Price Inflation Across the United States

With Michael Adjemian and Qingxiao Li · Supported by USDA NIFA

This project studies the supply and demand forces behind U.S. food price inflation. It examines how their contributions differ across local markets. The evidence comes from Circana retail scanner data covering more than 3,000 U.S. counties.

Presented at the SAEA Annual Meeting, Louisville, 2026.
